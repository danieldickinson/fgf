What Dominion Warzone Is

Dominion Warzone is a 6-day, server-versus-server event. Two entire servers compete: first by racking up points during a 5-day preparation phase, then by fighting a final cross-server battle on Day 6.

The winning server from the preparation phase becomes the Attacker. The losing server becomes the Defender. The Attacker gets to invade the Defender's Ascendancy Fortress on Battle Day — and tries to capture it before the timer runs out.

The Core Idea Score more preparation points than the enemy server → you attack on Saturday. Whoever controls the Ascendancy Fortress at the end wins.

Attacker vs Defender

Both roles can win — but they play very differently.

⚔️ Attacker

Server that wins the preparation point race.

  • Can jump into the defender's Ascendancy Fortress system
  • Uses Expedition Jump Points (requires Energy Core Lv 15+)
  • Wins by reaching 100% occupation on the Ascendancy Fortress before 18:00 UTC
  • Gets the initiative — picks when and where to push

🛡️ Defender

Server that loses the preparation point race.

  • Cannot enter the attacker's server
  • Must protect its own Ascendancy Fortress and Garrison Strongholds
  • Wins by simply preventing 100% occupation until the deadline
  • Home-field advantage — fights on familiar ground

The Key Buildings

Ascendancy Fortress

The central objective. The attacker must occupy the defender's fortress to 100% before the contest ends. Everything else exists to support or block this push.

Offensive Garrison Stronghold

Periodically attacks enemy-aligned fleets. Upgrading reduces cooldown and increases damage. On Saturday, attacker strongholds teleport to the defender's battlefield.

Defensive Garrison Stronghold

Boosts Defense for all fleets on the server when garrisoned. Upgrading increases server defense and garrison capacity. If captured by enemies, the defense bonus is temporarily disabled.

Expedition Jump Point

How attackers get into the enemy server. Tap "Enter Battlefield" to jump, "Withdraw" to return. Every jump triggers a 30-minute cooldown — don't waste them.

Critical Rule The first fleet to garrison a key building sets its maximum rally capacity. Don't let random low-power players enter major buildings first — assign your strongest rally leaders.

The Attacker Playbook

Your goal is not killing the most fleets. Your goal is reaching 100% occupation on the Ascendancy Fortress before 18:00 UTC.

Opening Stage (00:05–14:00)

  • Send scouts, not armies. Identify defender garrisons and rally patterns.
  • Confirm safe routes from jump points to the Fortress.
  • Keep your strongest fleets fresh — do not burn them in the morning.

Main Contest (14:00–18:00)

  • Centralize calls. One commander, one focus target at a time.
  • Take or neutralize key Garrison Strongholds first — they hammer your rallies if left alone.
  • Don't push the Fortress until the server can hold pressure on it.
  • Rotate damaged fleets out fast. Don't feed kills.
  • Watch occupation %, not the kill feed.

The Defender Playbook

Your goal is not winning every fight. It's simply preventing 100% occupation until 18:00 UTC. You win by surviving.

Opening Stage (00:05–14:00)

  • Garrison your Defensive Strongholds first, with your strongest rally leaders.
  • Keep the Fortress's first-garrison slot for a high-power rally leader (sets the rally cap).
  • Monitor enemy scouts — note who's testing your defenses.

Main Contest (14:00–18:00)

  • Hold the Fortress. Everything else is secondary.
  • Counter-attack attacker garrisons — if you flip one, their defense buff drops.
  • Don't chase. Don't let attackers bait you out of the Fortress with side targets.
  • Rotate damaged fleets. Reinforce the Fortress constantly.
  • Trade fleets for time. Every minute survived is points off their occupation push.

PVP Battle — Detailed Strategy

A deep look at how Defense, Garrison, and Attack actually interact during the Saturday PVP day — and the honest answer to "can our entire server rally as one?"

Mental Model Think of the Saturday battlefield as three interlocking systems: Defense (passive buffs from held buildings), Garrison (the fleets sitting inside those buildings), and Attack (the rallies and solo strikes pushing into enemy positions). Every coordination decision a server makes is really a decision about which of these three to spend on right now.

The Three Pillars: How They Interact

🛡️ Defense

Server-wide passive buffs and structural advantages. Defensive Garrison Strongholds grant a Defense bonus to every fleet on the server while held. Lose the building, lose the buff.

  • Stacks with Garrisoned-fleet default Defense bonus
  • Buff disabled if Stronghold is captured
  • Higher Stronghold level = larger buff + more garrison slots

⚓ Garrison

The fleets physically sitting inside a building. They take hits, return fire, and (critically) the first one in sets the rally cap. When any garrisoned fleet triggers War Frenzy, every other garrisoned fleet at that building gets the buff too.

  • First-garrison player sets the rally capacity ceiling
  • Defensive bonus applies automatically to garrisoned fleets
  • Garrison Leader can be hot-swapped using Trader Prince / Military Counselor titles — but only when the building isn't actively in combat

⚔️ Attack

Active rallies and solo strikes pushing into enemy positions. Offensive Garrison Strongholds auto-attack enemy-aligned fleets on a cooldown. Player rallies stack on top — that's how Fortress occupation moves.

  • Offensive Strongholds teleport to the defender's battlefield on Saturday
  • Solo attackers get cut down fast — rallies are the unit of force
  • Defeated rally participants still earn rewards if the rally wins

The Garrison System — The Detail That Decides Battles

Most servers lose Dominion not because their attack is weak, but because they fumble the Garrison system. The mechanics are unforgiving:

Rally Cap Is Set on First Garrison

When the first fleet enters a Stronghold or the Fortress, that player's rally size becomes the cap for every subsequent rally launched from that building. If a Level 9 player garrisons your Ascendancy Fortress first because they were excited, every Defender rally from that Fortress is capped at their rally size for the rest of Battle Day. Assign first-garrison explicitly — this is the single most common server-wide own goal.

War Frenzy Is Shared

If any single garrisoned fleet triggers a War Frenzy buff, every other fleet currently garrisoned at that same building inherits it. This means stacking high-power fleets in one Defensive Stronghold isn't just additive — it multiplies buff propagation across the whole garrison.

The Garrison Leader Title Trick

The Garrison Leader (whose fleet sets the rally cap) can be swapped out using the Trader Prince or Military Counselor titles — but only while the building is NOT in active combat. Plan title holders before the contest window. The standard pattern: a high-rally R4 holds Trader Prince so they can swap a fresh, high-power player into Garrison Leader between attack waves.

Defense Bonus Is Conditional

The Defensive Stronghold's server-wide Defense bonus is only active while your faction holds the building. The moment enemies flip it, the buff drops — even on fleets nowhere near the Stronghold. This is why Defender counter-attacks on flipped Strongholds are so valuable: flipping one back restores buffs across your whole server in the middle of a fight.

Can Guilds On the Same Server Truly Rally As One?

The Honest Answer Mechanically: no. A rally is launched by one player, from one building, into one target — and only players from the rally leader's guild can join that specific rally. There is no UI for an inter-guild rally where a player from Guild A reinforces a rally led by Guild B.

Strategically: yes. The event is server-vs-server, so guilds on the same server must coordinate or get curb-stomped — even though they fight inside their own rally chains.

What Cross-Guild Server Coordination Actually Looks Like

In practice, top servers run their PVP day through a shared coordination layer that sits outside the game:

  • Inter-guild Discord / Telegram. A shared comms server with reps (R5 + key R4s) from every major guild. This is where target priorities, timing, and "who pushes what" get decided.
  • One server-wide caller. Same principle as inside a guild — one voice does the calling for the whole server during the main contest. Usually the most experienced R5 across all guilds, by consensus.
  • Guild role specialization. Strong attack guilds run the rally pushes onto enemy Fortress. Mid-tier guilds run reinforcement waves and garrison rotation. Smaller guilds run scouting, jump-bait, and chip damage on Strongholds. Everyone has a real job.
  • Shared map / target board. A pinned image in Discord with current Stronghold ownership, next priority target, and who's leading the next push. Updated every 15–20 minutes during the contest window.

Where Guilds Can Actually Help Each Other Inside the Game

  • Garrison overlap. Members from multiple guilds can each individually garrison the same Stronghold. The first-garrison rule still applies, but stacking fleets across guilds inside one building boosts its survivability.
  • Reinforcement to held buildings. Any player on the server can reinforce a Stronghold their server holds, regardless of which guild captured it. This is the closest thing to a "server-wide rally."
  • Synchronized solo strikes. When two guilds independently launch rallies onto the same target in the same minute, the cumulative damage absolutely counts. Coordination is on timing, not on the rally itself.
What Doesn't Work Hoping rival guilds on your server "just figure it out" the day of. The strongest individual guild on a server cannot carry Dominion alone — an Ascendancy Fortress push requires sustained pressure from multiple rallies running in overlapping waves. If guild relationships are bad, neutralize them with a one-time diplomatic agreement before Day 1 of the event, or accept that you're going in as the Defender.

Attacker Composition & Wave Tactics

Your goal is reaching 100% occupation on the Defender's Ascendancy Fortress before 18:00 UTC. Every other action exists to support that single objective.

The Three Attack Roles

  1. Strike Captains (Burst DPS). Lead the rallies that hit the Fortress. Need high flagship power, fast rally times, and reliable presence during the 14:00–18:00 window. Run Beam-heavy for sustained DPS or Ion-heavy if Defenders are stacking Beam in their garrisons.
  2. Interceptors. Hunt Defender flagships moving between buildings. Their job is to break Defender counter-pushes before those fleets reach a Stronghold to garrison. Kinetic flagships excel here because of fast travel and burst.
  3. Support / Reinforcement. Run reinforcement fleets into Offensive Strongholds your server captured on the Defender's battlefield, and into rally chains as filler power. Mid-tier players make ideal supports — you need volume.

Wave Structure

  1. Wave 1 (14:00–14:30) — Open the corridor. Strike Captains break the closest Defender Strongholds to the Fortress. Don't push the Fortress yet — if you start the occupation meter while Strongholds still hammer your fleets, you bleed power for no progress.
  2. Wave 2 (14:30–15:30) — Pressure the Fortress. First sustained occupation rallies. Expect Defenders to counter-push hard. Interceptors clear counter-rallies in transit.
  3. Wave 3 (15:30–17:00) — Grind. Rotate rallies. Reset jump cooldowns. Keep occupation % climbing. Defender stamina starts to dip here.
  4. Wave 4 (17:00–18:00) — Closing push. This is where your held-back fresh fleet pays off. Strike Captains who skipped Wave 1 jump in now. Pile every available rally onto the Fortress for the last 60 minutes.
Reserve Discipline Hold at least 20% of your top-tier fleet capacity uncommitted until the Fortress meter reaches 60%+. The last 40% of occupation is contested far harder than the first 60% — that fresh wave is what closes the event.

Defender Composition & Holding Patterns

You don't need to win every fight. You need to survive to 18:00 UTC with Fortress occupation below 100%. Trade fleets for time.

The Three Defense Roles

  1. Fortress Anchor. The highest-power rally leader on the server. Garrisons the Ascendancy Fortress first (sets the max rally cap), and stays parked there for the entire contest window. Title: Garrison Leader.
  2. Stronghold Holders. One assigned rally leader per Defensive Stronghold, each with their own backup. They hold their building, run counter-rallies onto enemies that flipped neighboring buildings, and keep the server's Defense buff active.
  3. Counter-Push Mobile Force. Fast interceptor fleets — usually Kinetic — that don't garrison anything. Their job is to crush Attacker rallies the moment they form, before those rallies can leave the Defender's Jump Point landing zone.

The Hold Pattern

  1. Pre-contest (00:05–14:00). Anchor garrisons the Fortress. Stronghold Holders fill their assignments. Title holders staged. Mobile Force on standby near jump landing zones.
  2. Opening 30 minutes (14:00–14:30). Resist the urge to chase. Let the Attacker break the first Stronghold — you can flip it back later when their wave thins. Conserve fleets.
  3. Mid-contest (14:30–17:00). Run targeted counter-flips on Strongholds the Attacker captured. Each flip strips their Offensive buff and restores yours. Rotate damaged fleets through the Fortress — never let occupation pressure pause your reinforcement.
  4. Closing hour (17:00–18:00). All hands on Fortress. The Attacker is throwing reserves — you do the same. The clock is your weapon. Every minute survived denies them the win.
The Defender's Trick A flipped Offensive Stronghold doesn't just lose the Attacker their buff — the building follows them home when the battle ends, so they take a permanent step backward going into the next cycle. Late-contest flips are worth pursuing even if the Fortress is already safe.

The Cross-Guild Comms Playbook

If you take one thing from this section: build the comms layer before Day 1. Servers that wait until Battle Day to coordinate across guilds lose. Servers that have a working inter-guild Discord by the start of preparation phase don't.

What to set up the week before

  • One shared Discord (or Telegram). One channel for strategy, one for live calls during the contest, one pinned-info channel for target maps and role assignments.
  • One designated server commander. Picked by consensus from R5s across major guilds. Their voice carries during the 14:00–18:00 window. Backup commander identified.
  • Role assignments by guild. Each guild self-assigns which of their rally leaders fills which role (Strike Captain / Stronghold Holder / Mobile Force / Reinforcement). Names go on a shared sheet.
  • Energy Core Lv 15+ roster. Confirmed list of every player who can actually use Expedition Jump Points if your server becomes the Attacker.
  • Practice run. A coordinated rally onto a low-stakes target the day before, just to verify everyone is in the comms server and understands the call structure.

Live-Call Format During the Main Contest

Calls should be short and structured. The standard template every guild can drop into chat:

CALL: [target building] LEAD: [rally leader name + guild tag] ETA: [time rally fills] NEEDED: [reinforcement count] WAVE: [1 / 2 / 3 / 4]

One commander makes one call at a time. Everyone else follows. Side-channel debate happens in a separate channel; the main channel is for execution only.

3. Six Strategic Priorities for Leadership

① Control Spending Windows

The single biggest mistake: players spending key resources before the correct stage. Publish daily reminders: "Today is Stage 2 — spend Champion Fragments and Weapon Fragments now. Hold tech and flagship items."

② Push Server Points, Not Just Individual Chests

Individual milestones are good. But the server-wide score is what decides Attacker vs Defender. Even small players hitting their dailies adds millions of points across the server.

③ Identify High-Value Players Per Stage

Combat players matter most on Stage 1. Heavy spenders matter most on Stage 2. Tech hoarders and ruin players carry Stage 3. Flagship-focused players carry Stage 4. Match calls to the day.

④ Build a Real Battle-Day Roster

Track in advance — who can attend 14:00–18:00 UTC, who has Energy Core 15+, who leads rallies, who garrisons, who reinforces, who handles comms. "Enthusiasm" is not a plan.

⑤ Prevent Random First-Garrison Mistakes

The first player to station a fleet in a key building sets its rally capacity. If a low-power player garrisons first, your whole rally is capped at their level. Assign first-garrison roles explicitly.

⑥ Use Titles Properly

Trader Prince and Military Counselor titles can change a Garrison Leader while the building isn't in combat. Plan title assignments before Battle Day so the right person can hot-swap garrison leadership.

Final Principle

The winning server is not the one with the most powerful players. It's the one that:

  • Saves the right resources
  • Spends them on the right day
  • Coordinates its strongest players
  • Keeps its smaller players engaged
  • Controls key buildings properly
  • Shows up during the 14:00–18:00 UTC contest window

Treat Dominion Warzone as a server operation, not an individual event.

The Economy in One Picture

Everything in Foundation: Galactic Frontier ultimately runs on three flows:

  1. Trade output — your Flagship runs trade trips; trips produce Credits and resources.
  2. Guild output — your Guild's held Ports generate Resource Earnings claimable by every member.
  3. Event output — Dominion, Top 100, Arms Race, etc. pay out bundles, speedups, and premium items.

The Home Port multiplies trade output. Guild Ports multiply Guild output. Event spending discipline (the Dominion stockpile rule) multiplies event output. The best economic players plan all three together, not just whichever one is in front of them today.

Early Game (first 1–2 weeks)

  • Get in a Commerce Guild before you hit Energy Core Lv 5 — higher-tier Ports require it

Mid Game

  • Race your Home Port to L5+ to unlock higher trade attempts and daily reward tiers
  • Establish trade routes; expand them as soon as new systems open
  • Hold the highest-value consumables for Dominion stages instead of spending them as they drop
  • Start hoarding research speedups for event windows (Top 100, Arms Race)

Late Game

  • Push Home Port to L7–L9 for max Trade Attempts (cap at 40) and best DR tier
  • Co-occupy multiple Planetary Ports in systems where your Guild holds a Star Port — this maxes Resource Earnings
  • Time premium spending with anchor Champion rotations (Evan Rogers, Ajita, Zora windows)
  • Treat your speedup reserve as a strategic asset, not a routine consumable

All Stages

  • Claim daily rewards and Resource Earnings every day — unclaimed = lost
  • Don't spend premium consumables outside their matching Dominion stage

Fleet & Combat

Energy Types, champion synergy, flagship formations, and the tactical mechanics that decide every fight — built from FunPlus's Mastering Fleet Power guide and community formation playbooks.

How Combat Actually Works

A fleet in Foundation: Galactic Frontier is one Flagship plus up to three Champions. The Flagship sets the fleet's Energy Type (Beam, Kinetic, or Ionic), the Champions slot in alongside it, and almost every combat number you care about flows from how well these four pieces are aligned.

Three systems decide most fights:

  1. Energy Type matchup — a rock-paper-scissors triangle that grants a flat +5% damage when you have the advantage.
  2. Champion synergy — Champions of the same Energy Type as your Flagship grant +10%/+20% ATK, DEF, and INT to the whole fleet (at 2/3 matching).
  3. Formation activation order — Champion skills fire left-to-right, so debuffs and supports go first, damage dealers last.

The Four Combat Levers

Flagship

The command ship. Sets your fleet's Energy Type (Beam/Kinetic/Ionic) and runs a Style determined by its Core Component. Currently top picks: Gram (Beam, stat powerhouse), Demerzel (Ionic, control/debuff), Opportunity (Kinetic, auto-raid).

Champions

Up to three Champions per Flagship. Each has a passive (always on), an active (combat skill), and an ultimate (battle-changer). Champions of the matching Energy Type unlock their Flagship bonuses and stack synergy stats.

Core Component & Style

Your Flagship's Core Component determines its damage Style. Swapping Components lets you counter enemy Styles for a separate +5% damage bonus on top of the Energy Type advantage — stack both for up to +10%.

Formation Order

Skills activate left to right. Place supports and debuffers first so their buffs/debuffs land before your damage dealers swing. Rearranging the slot order is one of the highest-leverage free upgrades in the game.

The Big Idea Fleet power is multiplicative, not additive. Energy advantage (+5%) × synergy bonus (+20%) × Core Component advantage (+5%) × correct formation order is the difference between winning a fight and getting picked apart at equal raw power. Mid-tier players who master these multipliers regularly out-perform whales who don't.

The Energy Type Triangle

Every ship in the game specializes in one of three Energy Types. They counter each other in a strict rock-paper-scissors triangle. Hitting an enemy with the type you counter grants a flat +5% damage — small alone, decisive in close fights, devastating when stacked with Core Component advantage.

The Three Stats

Every Flagship and Champion contributes to three core stats. Understand what each one actually does before you spend on accessories.

ATK — Attack

Raw damage output. The headline stat for any DPS Champion or Beam/Kinetic-focused build. ATK scales basic attacks and the damage of abilities that read off ATK.

DEF — Defense

Reduces incoming damage. Doesn't show up on kill-feed posters, but DEF is what lets your formation outlast longer engagements (Port sieges, sustained Dominion contests). Stack on tanks.

INT — Intelligence

Boosts ability efficiency: damage of skill-based attacks, strength of heals, depth of debuffs. INT is what makes support and control Champions (Ajita, Demerzel, etc.) actually matter.

Formation Order — The Free Upgrade

Champion skills activate in the order they're placed in your formation, left to right. This single rule changes how you should build every formation.

The General Rule

  1. Slot 1 (leftmost): Debuffer or support. Their effect lands first and weakens the enemy / buffs your fleet before damage starts flowing.
  2. Slot 2: Sustain or secondary control. Healing and continuing pressure.
  3. Slot 3 (rightmost): Burst damage dealer. Their ultimate hits last and benefits from every buff/debuff above.
Concrete Example — Ionic Formation Slot 1: Ajita (debuff + heal lands first) → Slot 2: Jodie (sustained ATK flows in) → Slot 3: Lily (burst lands with both buffs active). Rearranging this order can swing fight outcomes more than a full champion level.

Target Selection

PVP & GVG

  • Lead with the Energy Type that counters the enemy's main fleet — that's a free +5%.
  • Engage from the front for full DPS. Rear-position attacks waste energy and lose burst potential.
  • Don't chase isolated trader ships in major fights — feeds the enemy and disrupts your own rallies.
  • For Port Wars, hold burst damage executors in reserve for the final hit. The guild that lands the killing blow wins — not the guild that does the most damage overall.

PVE Targets

  • Tribute Vessels — rally-coordinated. Massive Dominion points; never solo these.
  • Ascendancy Minions — solo farmable. Hit the highest level you can clear efficiently, not the highest level you can technically clear.
  • Shrines — primary auto-raid target. 3–4 per day at your highest manageable level is the standard farming cadence.
  • Ruins — Dominion gold during Stage 3. Plundering = +25K points each.

Auto-Raid Routine

Auto-Raid is how you keep your account growing while you're offline. The standard daily cadence:

  1. Run 3–4 shrine battles at the highest level your formation reliably clears. Shrine kills are the primary source of Guild Vouchers and daily mission rewards.
  2. Set Auto-Raid before logging off. Gram with a Kinetic INT/DEF team is the canonical farming setup, but any clearing-capable formation works.
  3. Don't over-stretch. If a level reliably wins but takes 90% of your fleet HP, drop one tier. Auto-Raid stops if a fight fails — losing one fight costs you the whole cycle.
  4. Check Components drops daily. Tribute Vessels and Ascendancy Shrines drop Flagship Components — these are the highest-impact upgrades you can stack between major investments.

Power Scaling — What Actually Grows Your Fleet

In order of return per hour/dollar invested:

  1. Match Champion type to Flagship. Free. Skipping this is the most expensive mistake in the game.
  2. Fix your formation order. Free. Move debuffs to slot 1, burst to slot 3.
  3. Carry alternate Core Components for Style swaps when content allows. Cheap, +5% damage per swap.
  4. Upgrade Flagship Components from the Glory Shop, Tribute Vessel drops, Ascendancy Shrines. Steady ATK/DEF/INT gains.
  5. Push your anchor Champion to next tier breakpoint. Tier jumps unlock new ability levels and big stat steps.
  6. Buy during your anchor's rotation window only. Evan Rogers / Ajita / Zora windows are when champion fragment efficiency is highest. Spending outside the window is reactive and wasteful.

Anti-Patterns

  • Building Champions of mismatched Energy Type — they don't unlock Flagship-specific bonuses.
  • Spending on modules before your Flagship type is locked in — modules are type-locked.
  • Over-investing in Phade after Ajita's rotation is on the horizon — Phade is a placeholder, not a long-term spine.
  • Putting your DPS Champion in slot 1 — their burst fires before debuffs land, wasting the multiplier.
  • Auto-Raiding at a level you barely clear — one loss kills the whole cycle.
  • Building only one Energy Type across the guild — coordinated opponents will counter you every Port War.
Bottom Line Pick a Flagship type with intent. Match all three Champion slots. Order them so supports fire first, damage fires last. Carry a Core Component swap when you can. Run Auto-Raid every night. The fleet that compounds these multipliers will out-perform a higher-power fleet that doesn't every single time.

Diplomacy & Alliances

Why Diplomacy Matters in FGF

Foundation: Galactic Frontier is a Guild game wearing a Trader skin. Almost everything past the first week — Port Wars, Dominion, the Xarnas competition, Top 100 ranking — is decided by Guild and inter-Guild relationships. The strongest individual account on a server cannot beat a coordinated mid-tier alliance.

Three diplomatic systems govern the meta:

  1. Commerce Guilds — your home unit. Members, officers, Chairman.
  2. Inter-Guild Alliances — the informal pacts (NAPs, attack-cooperation, shared Discord) between Guilds on the same server.
  3. The Xarnas Trade Prince Competition — the server-wide political event that decides which Guild gets decree power for a whole week.

The Diplomatic Stack

Within Your Guild

Internal coordination: rally roles, role assignments, who-does-what for Tribute rallies, Port Wars, and Battle Day. Run by Chairman + Executives.

Between Guilds (Same Server)

Inter-guild Discord, NAPs, shared maps, Xarnas alliances. The layer that decides whether your server out-coordinates the enemy on Dominion's Battle Day.

Cross-Server

Mostly relevant for Dominion Warzone (server-vs-server) and Top 100 leaderboards. Less day-to-day, but the decisive layer for major events.

The Hard Truth A server with three rival Guilds that refuse to coordinate will lose Dominion to a server with three Guilds that have a shared Discord and a designated server commander. Building inter-Guild relationships is not optional — it's the single highest-leverage thing R5s can do outside of recruiting.

  • Promote slowly. R4 permissions include Port War declarations — one impulsive R4 can wreck a week. Vet before promoting.
  • Have a code of conduct. Even a simple "join the Discord, follow calls, don't ghost wars" pinned message prevents most internal drama.

The Trade Prince Decree

Holding Xarnas gives the controlling Guild's Chairman a limited-use server-wide buff system. Decrees can:

  • Apply server-wide buffs to your Guild (ATK, DEF, resource rates depending on decree type)
  • Apply debuffs or restrictions to opposing Guilds
  • Adjust trade pricing or event modifiers

Recent patch notes increased the number of uses per Decree, and decree cooldowns no longer reset when Paths to Dominance begins on the server — meaning the Prince can carry decree budget into major events.

Strategic Implications

If You Win Xarnas

  • Stack decrees with major events — Dominion prep stages, Top 100 windows, Arms Race
  • Use decree budget early so rivals have less time to counter-position
  • Don't burn all decree uses on day 1 — save some for emergencies (e.g., a contested Port War)

If You Lose Xarnas

  • Expect your Guild to be debuff-targeted during the week
  • Plan major spending around the buff-week reset rather than during it
  • Start coordinating your next-week contest this week — the comms layer matters as much as the fleet

The Two-Front Problem

Don't Stretch Your Roster Xarnas PVP day routinely overlaps with Port War cycles. Trying to fight a Port War and contest Xarnas on the same day splits your top players. Decide which front matters more before the conflict, and either commit fully or stand down from one of them. Stretched servers lose both.

Reading the Server Power Map

Every competitive server has a power map: which Guilds hold Ports, who's allied with whom, who's contesting Xarnas, where the next Port War is likely. Top R5s read this map weekly. Below is the standard framework.

The Three Server Archetypes

  1. One-dominant. A single Guild controls most Star Ports, the Trade Prince, and dictates the meta. Other Guilds either ally up or accept second-tier. Stable but boring; risk of stagnation.
  2. Two-pole. Two large Guilds compete with smaller allied Guilds orbiting each. The most common state. Port Wars and Xarnas swing weekly.
  3. Fractured. Multiple mid-tier Guilds compete without clear hierarchy. Drama-heavy, low coordination, easy prey for any well-organized smaller Guild that consolidates.

When to Ally vs When to War

Ally When...

  • You share a common rival who outpowers either of you alone
  • You'd both benefit from coordinated Dominion / Xarnas pushes
  • The other Guild has timezone coverage you lack (or vice versa)
  • You're geographically adjacent and constant skirmishing burns both Guilds
  • The alliance unlocks Top 100 or Arms Race coordination

War When...

  • A Port War would meaningfully expand your Star Port count (and you can hold it)
  • An aggressive neighbor refuses reasonable NAP terms
  • You need to break a coalition that's locking your Guild out of Xarnas
  • A rival is materially weaker and consolidation is feasible without burning relationships you'll need later
Don't War for Pride Most regrettable wars start because someone got attacked, posted in chat about it, and the Chairman declared retaliation in 30 seconds. Sleep on it. Wars cost resources and burn alliances that take months to rebuild. If your gut says "personal grudge, not strategic gain" — it's not the time.

The Diplomatic Playbook

Building the Inter-Guild Comms Layer

  1. Set up a Discord. Or join an existing one. One server, one strategy channel, one live-calls channel, one map / role-assignment channel.
  2. Identify the other R5s. Reach out personally to the Chairman of every major Guild on your server. Even one in-game message starts the relationship.
  3. Pick a server commander. By consensus from R5s. Their voice carries during Dominion's main contest and major Port Wars.
  4. Define role specialization by Guild. Stronger Guilds run frontline rallies. Mid-tier Guilds run reinforcement. Smaller Guilds run scouting and chip damage. Everyone has a real job.
  5. Schedule a practice run. A coordinated rally onto a low-stakes target before the first major event. Confirms everyone is in comms and understands the call structure.

Handling Conflict

De-escalation Sequence

  1. Identify the actual grievance. Often it's one player on each side, not the Guild.
  2. R5-to-R5 conversation. Direct message. Not in Guild chat where the public watching forces posturing.
  3. Offer a face-saving concession. A small Port return, a public apology from the offending player, a temporary NAP.
  4. If de-escalation fails, define endgame. Don't drift into open hostility — commit to a finite goal (e.g., "we'll attack X Port next cycle and stop") so the conflict has a known endpoint.
The Long Game Server politics last longer than any single event. The R5 you war with this week may be the partner you need against a stronger common rival next month. Burn relationships sparingly — you can almost always replay a fight, but you can rarely replay a betrayal.

Coordinating With Allies for Dominion

The Dominion Warzone is where inter-Guild coordination pays off most concretely. The standard server-wide playbook:

  • Pre-event Discord pin: roles by Guild, target priorities, Energy Core Lv 15+ roster, server commander's contact info.
  • Daily check-in during prep: who's leading today's Tribute Vessel rallies; who's behind on commission completion; what stage opens tomorrow.
  • Battle Day live calls: single channel, single commander, structured call format (CALL / LEAD / ETA / NEEDED / WAVE). See the PVP Battle sub-section for the template.
  • Post-event hot wash: what worked, what didn't, who showed up, who didn't. Critical for retaining alliance trust into the next cycle.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • An ally Guild that promises Battle Day participation but skips practice runs
  • A neighboring Chairman who only responds to messages when they want something
  • A Guild that publicly trash-talks its own allies in server chat
  • An R5 who declares Port Wars without consulting their Executives
  • An ally that consistently shows up late or under-commits during major events
  • A Guild that breaks NAPs once and gives "miscommunication" as the explanation
Bottom Line Diplomacy in FGF is unglamorous, slow, and disproportionately impactful. The R5 who builds inter-Guild trust over months wins more big events than the R5 who optimizes their personal Flagship. The server with working comms wins. The server with three drama-soaked Guilds loses to a coordinated one every single time.

Tech & Research

The research priority order, why fleet combat always comes first, and how to time speedups for maximum event return. Built from community priority guides and the official patch language.

The Wrong-Tree Problem

The game does not tell you which research tree matters. Every node visually looks like progress. Three months into an account, the player who researched reactively — whatever was available, whatever felt productive — is two tiers behind the player who researched the right tree first, with the same spending and the same time investment.

The fix is uncomplicated and front-loaded: before you spend your first research speedup, commit to a priority order, and stick with it. Everything else fills in around it.

The Core Principle Right tree first. Right timing second. Every speedup spent on the wrong tree is roughly 2x cost — you spend it once on the wrong node, then later have to spend again to catch up on the right one. Get the order right from the start.

What Research Actually Does

FGF research branches across five broad categories. None are useless — but only some matter for competitive performance:

  • Fleet Combat — flagship ATK, DEF, HP. The performance tree.
  • Flagship Modules / Type-Specific — nodes that buff your specific Beam, Ion, or Kinetic kit.
  • Ground Combat — champion strike teams (shrines, daily missions).
  • Development — build speed, base infrastructure, gating unlocks.
  • Economy — Credit generation, resource efficiency.

The first two decide your competitive fate. The third feeds your daily activity floor. The last two fill in once those are humming.

Research Priority by Tier

The priority order that prevents the wrong-tree problem. Hit the first tier hard before touching the next.

Tier 1 — Fleet Combat Nodes (always first)

The fleet combat research tree is the highest-priority path in the game. Every node increases your Flagship's combat performance and translates directly into Port War wins, GVG results, and Dominion Battle Day performance.

Order within fleet combat:

  1. ATK nodes — scale every hit, also scale Ajita's healing (Ion) and Evan Rogers' formation buff (Beam) effectiveness
  2. DEF nodes — reduce incoming damage; matters most in extended engagements
  3. HP nodes — durability; extends how long your formation survives

Damage output is the multiplier. DEF and HP keep you in the fight long enough to apply that multiplier. ATK first, always.

Tier 2 — Flagship Type-Specific Research

After universal fleet combat nodes, move to research that buffs your specific flagship type (Beam, Ion, or Kinetic). A Beam-specific ATK node delivers more value to a Beam flagship than a universal ATK node at the same tier.

Critical Gate Type-specific research does not transfer if you switch flagship types. Confirm your Flagship type before investing here. See the Fleet & Combat section for type selection.

Tier 3 — Ground Combat Research

Ground combat governs your champion strike teams — the three-hero formation handling shrines, daily missions, and ground-based content. Two reasons it matters:

  • Shrine kills are your primary source of Guild Vouchers and daily mission rewards
  • Ground combat champion performance scales with research nodes as well as champion investment — under-researched ground combat costs daily activity returns every single day

Ground combat doesn't need to keep pace with fleet combat dollar-for-dollar. But neglecting it entirely creates a daily-activity gap that compounds across months.

Tier 4 — Development & Economy

These trees aren't useless — but they're not what wins events.

  • Development — prioritize only when a node is actively blocking a unlock you need (e.g., higher-tier flagship modules, more Guild Ports). Otherwise, defer.
  • Economy — passive Credit generation and resource efficiency. Meaningful for F2P, negligible for spenders. Lowest priority on a competitive account.

Flagship Type-Specific (Modules)

Tied to your specific Energy Type:

  • Beam research — buffs Beam flagship base stats, Beam ability damage
  • Ionic research — buffs Ionic flagship debuff effectiveness, control depth
  • Kinetic research — buffs Kinetic flagship burst damage, auto-raid efficiency

Each tier of type-specific research is meaningfully stronger than the equivalent universal node for that type's flagship. But the nodes do not transfer if you switch types — lock your Flagship type first.

Ground Combat

Buffs your three-hero champion strike team performance. The team that runs shrines, daily missions, landing missions, and other ground-based content.

Why It's Worth Researching

  • Shrine kill efficiency = Guild Vouchers + daily mission rewards
  • Ground combat ATK and HP nodes are routinely under-researched by players focused on fleet combat — under-investing here gives up daily compound returns
  • Landing missions during major events draw on this tree, not the fleet tree

Development

Governs base construction speed, resource production rates, and (most importantly) Core progression gating. Some development nodes gate access to higher-tier base functions you need to unlock specific flagship module tiers.

Rule: Research development nodes only when one is blocking a progression unlock you actually need. Don't chase the tree for its own sake.

Economy

Passive Credit generation and resource efficiency. At spend levels above F2P, the speed improvement is negligible — your main resource bottleneck is premium consumable timing (Dominion stages, anchor Champion rotations), not passive flow. Last priority.

Research Speedups — When to Burn

Research speedups are the most valuable speedup type in FGF because research completions score efficiently in Top 100 Galactic Traders and Arms Race. Burning them passively between events is the compounded version of the wrong-tree mistake: wrong priority and wrong timing simultaneously.

The Speedup Discipline

  1. Don't burn passively. Between events, queue research and let it tick naturally. Bank your speedup reserve.
  2. Identify the next major event. Top 100 Galactic Traders, Arms Race, Dominion Warzone Stage 3 (the Technology & Crew day) all reward research completions.
  3. Burn during the event window only. Speedups used during a scoring window produce both permanent advancement and event points — double return.
  4. Match Dominion Stage 3 specifically. Every minute of Technology Speedup is worth +16 points during that stage. The same minute used the day before is worth 0.
  5. Hold a reserve. Keep at least 25% of your speedup bank uncommitted between events — sudden Top 100 windows or surprise Arms Race weekends can pay out unexpectedly well.

Speedup Pack Hoarding

The standard discipline at competitive spend levels:

  • Universal speedup bundles — banked, used during events that score completions
  • Research-specific speedup packs — banked, used only for fleet combat research during scoring windows
  • Build speedups — less event-impactful; can be burned more freely on progression unlocks
The Reactive Trap The most common mistake: a research node finishes, the game prompts "Speed up next?", the player taps yes out of momentum. That habit alone is the difference between Top 100 placement and missing the cutoff. Set the personal rule: speedups only during scoring events.

Common Questions

Does research reset if I switch Flagship types?

General fleet combat research carries over regardless of type. Flagship type-specific research nodes do not — they're tied to the type you researched them for. This is the strongest reason to lock your Flagship type before committing to type-specific paths.

How long does the fleet combat tree take to max?

Depth varies by server age and update cycle. At consistent spending with research speedups timed to event windows, expect the primary fleet combat path to take multiple months to fully advance. Planning speedup staging around Top 100 and Arms Race windows accelerates effective progress versus passive research alone by a meaningful margin.

Is there a node that's commonly overlooked but high-value?

Ground combat ATK and HP nodes. Players who hyper-focus on fleet combat routinely under-research these, losing daily shrine-kill efficiency and mission returns that compound across months. They're not a priority over fleet combat, but neglecting them entirely is a quiet long-term cost.

Do research completions always score in Top 100?

Yes — this is the primary reason to hoard research speedups for event windows rather than burning them passively between events. Top 100 Galactic Traders rewards completions, not just queued research.

I joined a server late — can I catch up on research?

Catch-up is real but slow. Two levers help:

  • Don't research reactively. Skip the temptation to fill out development/economy — route all speedups into fleet combat ATK first.
  • Hit every event scoring window. Even if you're behind, an event-aligned speedup burn closes the gap faster than equivalent passive progression.

What doesn't help: spreading speedups across multiple trees to look "balanced." That's the wrong-tree problem in slow motion.

What's the single biggest research mistake?

Going deep into development or economy nodes in the first few weeks because they're available and the completion feedback feels productive. Three months later, the player has a competitive flagship formation but fleet combat research two tiers behind a server peer who spent the same money but prioritized differently. The power gap is real and closing it requires burning speedups into catch-up research rather than forward progress.

The One-Line Version ATK in fleet combat, then DEF, then HP. Then your Flagship type's specific nodes. Then ground combat. Then development only when it's blocking something. Economy last. Speedups stay banked until an event scores them.
