Dominion Warzone

A complete, easy-to-follow guide to the 6-day server-vs-server event — scoring, daily stages, Battle Day, and how to win.

What Dominion Warzone Is

Dominion Warzone is a 6-day, server-versus-server event. Two entire servers compete: first by racking up points during a 5-day preparation phase, then by fighting a final cross-server battle on Day 6.

The winning server from the preparation phase becomes the Attacker. The losing server becomes the Defender. The Attacker gets to invade the Defender's Ascendancy Fortress on Battle Day — and tries to capture it before the timer runs out.

The Core Idea Score more preparation points than the enemy server → you attack on Saturday. Whoever controls the Ascendancy Fortress at the end wins.

Attacker vs Defender

Both roles can win — but they play very differently.

⚔️ Attacker

Server that wins the preparation point race.

  • Can jump into the defender's Ascendancy Fortress system
  • Uses Expedition Jump Points (requires Energy Core Lv 15+)
  • Wins by reaching 100% occupation on the Ascendancy Fortress before 18:00 UTC
  • Gets the initiative — picks when and where to push

🛡️ Defender

Server that loses the preparation point race.

  • Cannot enter the attacker's server
  • Must protect its own Ascendancy Fortress and Garrison Strongholds
  • Wins by simply preventing 100% occupation until the deadline
  • Home-field advantage — fights on familiar ground

The Key Buildings

Ascendancy Fortress

The central objective. The attacker must occupy the defender's fortress to 100% before the contest ends. Everything else exists to support or block this push.

Offensive Garrison Stronghold

Periodically attacks enemy-aligned fleets. Upgrading reduces cooldown and increases damage. On Saturday, attacker strongholds teleport to the defender's battlefield.

Defensive Garrison Stronghold

Boosts Defense for all fleets on the server when garrisoned. Upgrading increases server defense and garrison capacity. If captured by enemies, the defense bonus is temporarily disabled.

Expedition Jump Point

How attackers get into the enemy server. Tap "Enter Battlefield" to jump, "Withdraw" to return. Every jump triggers a 30-minute cooldown — don't waste them.

Critical Rule The first fleet to garrison a key building sets its maximum rally capacity. Don't let random low-power players enter major buildings first — assign your strongest rally leaders.

Cross-Server Jump Rules

Requirements

  • Player must be on the attacking server
  • Player must have Energy Core Level 15+

How It Works

  • Tap Enter Battlefield from the event interface to jump to the defender's Ascendancy Fortress system
  • Tap Withdraw from Battlefield to return home
  • Every jump = 30-minute cooldown. Don't burn it on scouting if you'll need it for the contest window

Restrictions

  • You can't use the defender's Star Gate to travel to other systems
  • Idle attacking fleets may auto-return home
  • Fleets inactive too long without Auto Rally may be temporarily hidden
  • At 24:00 UTC, all foreign players are forcibly recalled

The Attacker Playbook

Your goal is not killing the most fleets. Your goal is reaching 100% occupation on the Ascendancy Fortress before 18:00 UTC.

Opening Stage (00:05–14:00)

  • Send scouts, not armies. Identify defender garrisons and rally patterns.
  • Confirm safe routes from jump points to the Fortress.
  • Keep your strongest fleets fresh — do not burn them in the morning.

Main Contest (14:00–18:00)

  • Centralize calls. One commander, one focus target at a time.
  • Take or neutralize key Garrison Strongholds first — they hammer your rallies if left alone.
  • Don't push the Fortress until the server can hold pressure on it.
  • Rotate damaged fleets out fast. Don't feed kills.
  • Watch occupation %, not the kill feed.

The Defender Playbook

Your goal is not winning every fight. It's simply preventing 100% occupation until 18:00 UTC. You win by surviving.

Opening Stage (00:05–14:00)

  • Garrison your Defensive Strongholds first, with your strongest rally leaders.
  • Keep the Fortress's first-garrison slot for a high-power rally leader (sets the rally cap).
  • Monitor enemy scouts — note who's testing your defenses.

Main Contest (14:00–18:00)

  • Hold the Fortress. Everything else is secondary.
  • Counter-attack attacker garrisons — if you flip one, their defense buff drops.
  • Don't chase. Don't let attackers bait you out of the Fortress with side targets.
  • Rotate damaged fleets. Reinforce the Fortress constantly.
  • Trade fleets for time. Every minute survived is points off their occupation push.

PVP Battle — Detailed Strategy

A deep look at how Defense, Garrison, and Attack actually interact during the Saturday PVP day — and the honest answer to "can our entire server rally as one?"

Mental Model Think of the Saturday battlefield as three interlocking systems: Defense (passive buffs from held buildings), Garrison (the fleets sitting inside those buildings), and Attack (the rallies and solo strikes pushing into enemy positions). Every coordination decision a server makes is really a decision about which of these three to spend on right now.

The Three Pillars: How They Interact

🛡️ Defense

Server-wide passive buffs and structural advantages. Defensive Garrison Strongholds grant a Defense bonus to every fleet on the server while held. Lose the building, lose the buff.

  • Stacks with Garrisoned-fleet default Defense bonus
  • Buff disabled if Stronghold is captured
  • Higher Stronghold level = larger buff + more garrison slots

⚓ Garrison

The fleets physically sitting inside a building. They take hits, return fire, and (critically) the first one in sets the rally cap. When any garrisoned fleet triggers War Frenzy, every other garrisoned fleet at that building gets the buff too.

  • First-garrison player sets the rally capacity ceiling
  • Defensive bonus applies automatically to garrisoned fleets
  • Garrison Leader can be hot-swapped using Trader Prince / Military Counselor titles — but only when the building isn't actively in combat

⚔️ Attack

Active rallies and solo strikes pushing into enemy positions. Offensive Garrison Strongholds auto-attack enemy-aligned fleets on a cooldown. Player rallies stack on top — that's how Fortress occupation moves.

  • Offensive Strongholds teleport to the defender's battlefield on Saturday
  • Solo attackers get cut down fast — rallies are the unit of force
  • Defeated rally participants still earn rewards if the rally wins

The Garrison System — The Detail That Decides Battles

Most servers lose Dominion not because their attack is weak, but because they fumble the Garrison system. The mechanics are unforgiving:

Rally Cap Is Set on First Garrison

When the first fleet enters a Stronghold or the Fortress, that player's rally size becomes the cap for every subsequent rally launched from that building. If a Level 9 player garrisons your Ascendancy Fortress first because they were excited, every Defender rally from that Fortress is capped at their rally size for the rest of Battle Day. Assign first-garrison explicitly — this is the single most common server-wide own goal.

War Frenzy Is Shared

If any single garrisoned fleet triggers a War Frenzy buff, every other fleet currently garrisoned at that same building inherits it. This means stacking high-power fleets in one Defensive Stronghold isn't just additive — it multiplies buff propagation across the whole garrison.

The Garrison Leader Title Trick

The Garrison Leader (whose fleet sets the rally cap) can be swapped out using the Trader Prince or Military Counselor titles — but only while the building is NOT in active combat. Plan title holders before the contest window. The standard pattern: a high-rally R4 holds Trader Prince so they can swap a fresh, high-power player into Garrison Leader between attack waves.

Defense Bonus Is Conditional

The Defensive Stronghold's server-wide Defense bonus is only active while your faction holds the building. The moment enemies flip it, the buff drops — even on fleets nowhere near the Stronghold. This is why Defender counter-attacks on flipped Strongholds are so valuable: flipping one back restores buffs across your whole server in the middle of a fight.

Can Guilds On the Same Server Truly Rally As One?

The Honest Answer Mechanically: no. A rally is launched by one player, from one building, into one target — and only players from the rally leader's guild can join that specific rally. There is no UI for an inter-guild rally where a player from Guild A reinforces a rally led by Guild B.

Strategically: yes. The event is server-vs-server, so guilds on the same server must coordinate or get curb-stomped — even though they fight inside their own rally chains.

What Cross-Guild Server Coordination Actually Looks Like

In practice, top servers run their PVP day through a shared coordination layer that sits outside the game:

  • Inter-guild Discord / Telegram. A shared comms server with reps (R5 + key R4s) from every major guild. This is where target priorities, timing, and "who pushes what" get decided.
  • One server-wide caller. Same principle as inside a guild — one voice does the calling for the whole server during the main contest. Usually the most experienced R5 across all guilds, by consensus.
  • Guild role specialization. Strong attack guilds run the rally pushes onto enemy Fortress. Mid-tier guilds run reinforcement waves and garrison rotation. Smaller guilds run scouting, jump-bait, and chip damage on Strongholds. Everyone has a real job.
  • Shared map / target board. A pinned image in Discord with current Stronghold ownership, next priority target, and who's leading the next push. Updated every 15–20 minutes during the contest window.

Where Guilds Can Actually Help Each Other Inside the Game

  • Garrison overlap. Members from multiple guilds can each individually garrison the same Stronghold. The first-garrison rule still applies, but stacking fleets across guilds inside one building boosts its survivability.
  • Reinforcement to held buildings. Any player on the server can reinforce a Stronghold their server holds, regardless of which guild captured it. This is the closest thing to a "server-wide rally."
  • Synchronized solo strikes. When two guilds independently launch rallies onto the same target in the same minute, the cumulative damage absolutely counts. Coordination is on timing, not on the rally itself.
What Doesn't Work Hoping rival guilds on your server "just figure it out" the day of. The strongest individual guild on a server cannot carry Dominion alone — an Ascendancy Fortress push requires sustained pressure from multiple rallies running in overlapping waves. If guild relationships are bad, neutralize them with a one-time diplomatic agreement before Day 1 of the event, or accept that you're going in as the Defender.

Attacker Composition & Wave Tactics

Your goal is reaching 100% occupation on the Defender's Ascendancy Fortress before 18:00 UTC. Every other action exists to support that single objective.

The Three Attack Roles

  1. Strike Captains (Burst DPS). Lead the rallies that hit the Fortress. Need high flagship power, fast rally times, and reliable presence during the 14:00–18:00 window. Run Beam-heavy for sustained DPS or Ion-heavy if Defenders are stacking Beam in their garrisons.
  2. Interceptors. Hunt Defender flagships moving between buildings. Their job is to break Defender counter-pushes before those fleets reach a Stronghold to garrison. Kinetic flagships excel here because of fast travel and burst.
  3. Support / Reinforcement. Run reinforcement fleets into Offensive Strongholds your server captured on the Defender's battlefield, and into rally chains as filler power. Mid-tier players make ideal supports — you need volume.

Wave Structure

  1. Wave 1 (14:00–14:30) — Open the corridor. Strike Captains break the closest Defender Strongholds to the Fortress. Don't push the Fortress yet — if you start the occupation meter while Strongholds still hammer your fleets, you bleed power for no progress.
  2. Wave 2 (14:30–15:30) — Pressure the Fortress. First sustained occupation rallies. Expect Defenders to counter-push hard. Interceptors clear counter-rallies in transit.
  3. Wave 3 (15:30–17:00) — Grind. Rotate rallies. Reset jump cooldowns. Keep occupation % climbing. Defender stamina starts to dip here.
  4. Wave 4 (17:00–18:00) — Closing push. This is where your held-back fresh fleet pays off. Strike Captains who skipped Wave 1 jump in now. Pile every available rally onto the Fortress for the last 60 minutes.
Reserve Discipline Hold at least 20% of your top-tier fleet capacity uncommitted until the Fortress meter reaches 60%+. The last 40% of occupation is contested far harder than the first 60% — that fresh wave is what closes the event.

Defender Composition & Holding Patterns

You don't need to win every fight. You need to survive to 18:00 UTC with Fortress occupation below 100%. Trade fleets for time.

The Three Defense Roles

  1. Fortress Anchor. The highest-power rally leader on the server. Garrisons the Ascendancy Fortress first (sets the max rally cap), and stays parked there for the entire contest window. Title: Garrison Leader.
  2. Stronghold Holders. One assigned rally leader per Defensive Stronghold, each with their own backup. They hold their building, run counter-rallies onto enemies that flipped neighboring buildings, and keep the server's Defense buff active.
  3. Counter-Push Mobile Force. Fast interceptor fleets — usually Kinetic — that don't garrison anything. Their job is to crush Attacker rallies the moment they form, before those rallies can leave the Defender's Jump Point landing zone.

The Hold Pattern

  1. Pre-contest (00:05–14:00). Anchor garrisons the Fortress. Stronghold Holders fill their assignments. Title holders staged. Mobile Force on standby near jump landing zones.
  2. Opening 30 minutes (14:00–14:30). Resist the urge to chase. Let the Attacker break the first Stronghold — you can flip it back later when their wave thins. Conserve fleets.
  3. Mid-contest (14:30–17:00). Run targeted counter-flips on Strongholds the Attacker captured. Each flip strips their Offensive buff and restores yours. Rotate damaged fleets through the Fortress — never let occupation pressure pause your reinforcement.
  4. Closing hour (17:00–18:00). All hands on Fortress. The Attacker is throwing reserves — you do the same. The clock is your weapon. Every minute survived denies them the win.
The Defender's Trick A flipped Offensive Stronghold doesn't just lose the Attacker their buff — the building follows them home when the battle ends, so they take a permanent step backward going into the next cycle. Late-contest flips are worth pursuing even if the Fortress is already safe.

The Cross-Guild Comms Playbook

If you take one thing from this section: build the comms layer before Day 1. Servers that wait until Battle Day to coordinate across guilds lose. Servers that have a working inter-guild Discord by the start of preparation phase don't.

What to set up the week before

  • One shared Discord (or Telegram). One channel for strategy, one for live calls during the contest, one pinned-info channel for target maps and role assignments.
  • One designated server commander. Picked by consensus from R5s across major guilds. Their voice carries during the 14:00–18:00 window. Backup commander identified.
  • Role assignments by guild. Each guild self-assigns which of their rally leaders fills which role (Strike Captain / Stronghold Holder / Mobile Force / Reinforcement). Names go on a shared sheet.
  • Energy Core Lv 15+ roster. Confirmed list of every player who can actually use Expedition Jump Points if your server becomes the Attacker.
  • Practice run. A coordinated rally onto a low-stakes target the day before, just to verify everyone is in the comms server and understands the call structure.

Live-Call Format During the Main Contest

Calls should be short and structured. The standard template every guild can drop into chat:

CALL: [target building] LEAD: [rally leader name + guild tag] ETA: [time rally fills] NEEDED: [reinforcement count] WAVE: [1 / 2 / 3 / 4]

One commander makes one call at a time. Everyone else follows. Side-channel debate happens in a separate channel; the main channel is for execution only.

3. Six Strategic Priorities for Leadership

① Control Spending Windows

The single biggest mistake: players spending key resources before the correct stage. Publish daily reminders: "Today is Stage 2 — spend Champion Fragments and Weapon Fragments now. Hold tech and flagship items."

② Push Server Points, Not Just Individual Chests

Individual milestones are good. But the server-wide score is what decides Attacker vs Defender. Even small players hitting their dailies adds millions of points across the server.

③ Identify High-Value Players Per Stage

Combat players matter most on Stage 1. Heavy spenders matter most on Stage 2. Tech hoarders and ruin players carry Stage 3. Flagship-focused players carry Stage 4. Match calls to the day.

④ Build a Real Battle-Day Roster

Track in advance — who can attend 14:00–18:00 UTC, who has Energy Core 15+, who leads rallies, who garrisons, who reinforces, who handles comms. "Enthusiasm" is not a plan.

⑤ Prevent Random First-Garrison Mistakes

The first player to station a fleet in a key building sets its rally capacity. If a low-power player garrisons first, your whole rally is capped at their level. Assign first-garrison roles explicitly.

⑥ Use Titles Properly

Trader Prince and Military Counselor titles can change a Garrison Leader while the building isn't in combat. Plan title assignments before Battle Day so the right person can hot-swap garrison leadership.

Final Principle

The winning server is not the one with the most powerful players. It's the one that:

  • Saves the right resources
  • Spends them on the right day
  • Coordinates its strongest players
  • Keeps its smaller players engaged
  • Controls key buildings properly
  • Shows up during the 14:00–18:00 UTC contest window

Treat Dominion Warzone as a server operation, not an individual event.
